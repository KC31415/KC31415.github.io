Nasdaq (NDAQ): Long, $116 target

As of the 18 September 2026 close · price $93.54 · pitch dated 19 September 2026

Buy, with a 12-month price target of $116, 24% above the $93.54 close on 18 September 2026.

First presented during my summer 2026 internship; rebuilt from public sources in September 2026. Sources: company filings and earnings calls, Bloomberg, LSEG Workspace.

Thesis

In the February 2026 selloff in software and data stocks, the market priced in a risk that AI would erode Nasdaq’s business. Nasdaq’s operating data doesn’t show that damage: in Q2 2026 FinTech ARR grew 16%, Verafin revenue grew 21%, and management said sales cycles were unchanged. The stock still trades like an exchange, at 20.7x forward earnings and 1.07x the US exchange average, even though 77% of its net revenue comes from software, data, index licensing and listings rather than trading. I expect part of the February de-rating to reverse as the Q3 and Q4 2026 results and 2027 guidance show recurring growth holding through known tough comparisons.

Why the market had it wrong

It sold Nasdaq with the data names, not the exchanges

In the week of the AI-driven selloff, from 30 January to 6 February 2026, Nasdaq’s forward P/E fell 12.7%. That is in line with the data and analytics companies. Its exchange peers held up: ICE’s multiple fell 3.5%, while CME’s and Cboe’s rose.

It still trades like an exchange

By 18 September Nasdaq traded at 20.7x forward earnings. That is the 20th percentile of its 3-year range and 1.07x the average of ICE, CME and Cboe. Information-services peers trade higher.

77% of revenue being non-trading is not the same as 77% being recurring. Contractual ARR, $3.26bn and up 12%, is more than half of annualized net revenue; the rest of the non-trading revenue is mainly asset-based index fees and listings.

LSEG StarMine estimates that the price implies long-term EPS growth of 11.1%, against an I/B/E/S analyst estimate of 17.1%. The StarMine figure rests on its own valuation assumptions, so I read it as consistent with my view rather than proof of it. Part of the compression since February was sector-wide, which is why I assume only a partial reversal.

What the results show

If AI were taking business from Nasdaq’s software, the Q2 2026 numbers would show it in bookings, retention or growth. They don’t.

  • FinTech ARR grew 16%. This is the Financial Technology segment only; ARR across the company grew 12%.
  • Verafin revenue grew 21%, to $98m in the quarter, with 110% net revenue retention. Verafin’s 11 enterprise signings year to date already exceed all of 2025.
  • Sales cycles are unchanged, according to management. On the Q2 2026 earnings call, management said sales cycles, contract lengths and bookings mix had not changed.

I assume no material AI revenue. The thesis rests on demand holding up, not on Nasdaq making money from AI.

Where the return comes from

About half comes from earnings and half from the multiple. At today’s multiple, September 2027 forward EPS alone would put the stock at $106, up 13%. Re-rating to 22.7x adds the other 11 points.

Risks and exit triggers

AI disruption of FinTech and data
Slower bookings, pricing pressure or churn at Verafin or eVestment. I watch sales cycles, net revenue retention and ARR.
Perpetual futures and prediction markets
Volume migration, or another sentiment de-rating like June 2026’s. Management estimates direct revenue overlap at under 1% of revenue even in an extreme case.
Index sensitivity to markets
A 10% fall in index AUM would cut revenue by about $87m (about $0.09 of EPS).
Q3 and Q4 2026 optics
Tough upfront-revenue comparisons in Capital Markets Technology will slow reported FinTech growth. ARR is unaffected, so I anchor on ARR.
Expense creep
2026 expense guidance has been raised twice. The test is 2027 guidance against the 5–8% framework.
AI monetization unproven
Nasdaq doesn’t report AI revenue separately, and without it the re-rating could stall.

Deck, section D, lists four more: the 23-hour trading launch, options capture and volumes, Reg NMS changes, and rates.

I would exit or reverse the position if

  • FinTech organic ARR growth falls below 13% for two consecutive quarters;
  • Financial Crime revenue growth is still at or below its ~17% ARR growth by the Q4 2026 print (no enterprise conversion);
  • 2027 expense guidance implies growth above 8% without matching revenue upside;
  • the premium to US exchanges narrows below 1.00x after two in-line prints; or
  • the stock closes sustainably below the June 2026 low of $76.55, roughly a 1.5-standard-deviation three-month move. That triggers a full re-underwriting, and I exit unless fundamentals explain the move.

On the upside, I would reassess if the stock reaches about 25x forward earnings, its pre-selloff level, without a matching improvement in fundamentals.

What’s happened since

Updated 6 November 2026. The figures above are unchanged from the original pitch.

The stock closed at $106.83 on 5 November 2026, up 14% from $93.54 and a little more than halfway to the $116 target. About half the move came on 22 October, the day of the Q3 results, when it rose 7.5%.

The Q3 results support the thesis:

  • FinTech ARR grew 18%, a record for Nasdaq, in the quarter I expected tough Calypso comparisons to make FinTech look weaker. That is well clear of the 13% exit trigger. ARR across the company grew 12% (14% organic).
  • Financial Crime (Verafin) revenue grew 23%, and Nasdaq said its ARR growth accelerated, so the second exit trigger hasn’t fired. Enterprise signings reached 12 for the year, against 9 in all of 2025.
  • Management again said sales cycles, contract term lengths and bookings mix were consistent.
  • Non-GAAP EPS was $1.04, up 18%.

One point against: Nasdaq raised its 2026 expense guidance again, to $2.550–2.580bn, against the $2,550m in my model. It cited a recent acquisition and stronger revenue. Next I’m watching the 2027 expense guidance with the Q4 results in late January. Growth above 8% without matching revenue would be an exit trigger; guidance inside 5–8% with Solutions growth in or above 9–12% is the catalyst I expect to close the rest of the gap to $116.

Sources: Nasdaq Q3 2026 earnings release and call, 22 October 2026; closing prices from Yahoo Finance.
